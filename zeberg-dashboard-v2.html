Chicago Fed CFNAI
Broad activity, >0 = growth
--
loading

The Chicago Fed National Activity Index (CFNAI) is a "health gauge of the US economy". Think of it as a dashboard warning light showing how hard the engine is running — not for one company, but the entire economy. It combines 85 different economic signals into one number: employment, production, housing, sales, inventories, income, and more.

How do you read it?

0 = normal — Economy running at its historical average.
Above 0 — Economy running faster than normal.
Below 0 — Economy running slower than normal.

Easy reference:

+0.5 → "Economy feeling quite healthy."
0 → "Everything normal."
-0.5 → "Economy catching a cold."
-0.7 or lower (sustained) → "Watch out — recession risk rising."

Why investors care:

Strong (positive) → companies earn more → good for stocks, but central bank keeps rates high.
Weak (negative) → rate cuts become likely → sometimes good for tech/crypto, bad if recession looms.

💡 Tip: don't look at one month. Use the 3-month moving average (CFNAI-MA3) to see the real trend.

Industrial Production is America's factory and output thermometer. Are factories actually making things, are machines running, is energy being consumed?

What does it measure?

Output across: cars 🚗, machinery ⚙️, chips 💻, steel 🏗️, energy ⚡, mining ⛏️.
Rising → companies producing more.
Falling → economy cooling.

Capacity Utilization — even more interesting:

Think of a restaurant: 50% = half-empty. 80% = busy, running well. 95% = staff stretched, fully packed.

How do you read it?

80%+ → Strong, businesses running hard.
75%–80% → Normal / slightly soft.
Below 75% → Warning: slowdown or recession risk.
85%+ → Running too hot — inflation risk.

Why investors care:

Strong production + high utilization → good for cyclical stocks, but Fed keeps rates high longer.
Falling production → more chance of rate cuts → often favorable for tech/crypto.

Nonfarm Payrolls
MoM change (K jobs)
--
loading

Non Farm Payrolls (NFP) asks: "How many new jobs were added in America last month?" — America's job counter.

Why "non-farm"?

Farm jobs fluctuate too much seasonally. Military and self-employed also excluded. Yet this still covers ~80% of all US jobs.

How do you read it?

+250K → Companies hiring massively. Economy strong.
+150K → Healthy, normal growth.
+50K → Economy slowing.
0 or negative → Alarm bell. Recession risk rising.

For stocks and crypto:

Very strong NFP → good for banks/industry, but rates stay high — less good for tech/crypto.
Slightly weaker NFP → often bullish for Nasdaq and crypto (rate cut coming).
Very bad NFP → panic → stocks down.

💡 More jobs = stronger economy. But too strong keeps rates high — bad for tech/crypto.

Retail Sales YoY is America's spending meter: are Americans spending more or less than the same month last year?

Consumers are the engine of the US economy — over 70% of the economy runs on what people buy: clothing 👕, cars 🚗, electronics 💻, restaurants 🍔, online shopping 📦.

How do you read it?

+5%+ → People spending freely. Economy strong.
+2%–4% → Healthy, normal growth.
~0% → People becoming cautious.
Negative → People buying less — economic warning.

The catch:

Too strong → Fed thinks "too hot → inflation stays" → rates high longer → bad for Nasdaq/crypto.

💡 When Americans stop shopping, the economy usually starts coughing.

Labor Market — Zeberg "Under Water" Monitor

Unemployment Rate
Lagging, but crucial
--
loading

The Unemployment Rate (UNRATE) measures how many people want to work but don't have a job.

How do you read it?

3%–4% → Very strong economy.
4%–5% → Healthy, normal economy.
5%–7% → Economy cooling.
8%+ → Major problems.
10%+ → Crisis level (like 2008 or Covid).

Rising unemployment isn't always bad:

Slight rise → market thinks "Fed will cut rates" → often bullish for Nasdaq and crypto.
Too much rise → recession fears → bearish for almost everything.

Difference from NFP:

NFP = how many jobs added (car's speed 🚗)
UNRATE = how many people are jobless (engine temp 🌡️)

💡 Low unemployment = strong economy, but too low can cause inflation and hurt tech/crypto.

IC4WSA is America's layoff meter. Each week, the number of people filing for unemployment benefits for the first time is counted. The 4-week average filters out noise from snowstorms ❄️, strikes 🚧, or holidays 🎄 to show the real trend.

How do you read it?

180K–220K → Few layoffs, strong labor market.
250K–300K → Companies becoming cautious.
Hard rise above 300K → Alarm 🚨 — early recession warning.

IC4WSA is often the first smoke alarm — it's weekly and much faster than NFP or UNRATE.

For stocks and crypto:

Falling claims → bullish for stocks.
Slightly rising → rate cut closer → bullish for Nasdaq/crypto.
Exploding claims → recession fear → bearish for everything.

💡 When IC4WSA rises fast, the economy starts coughing before you see it elsewhere.

Sahm Rule Indicator
≥ 0.5 = recession trigger
--
loading

SAHMREALTIME is America's recession smoke alarm — invented by economist Claudia Sahm. The question: is unemployment suddenly rising faster?

The Sahm Rule:

When unemployment rises 0.5% or more above its lowest point of the past year, history says a recession has often already begun.
Example: lowest rate = 3.5% → alarm triggers at 4.0%.

How do you read the value?

Below 0.3 → Nothing alarming.
Toward 0.5 → ⚠️ Warning.
0.5 or higher → 🚨 Alarm — historically often already in recession.

⚠️ Nuance — 2024 lesson:

In 2024 the alarm briefly triggered without a real recession. Even Claudia Sahm said: "Take it easy, this time could be different."
Always use together with IC4WSA and UNRATE — never in isolation.

💡 SAHMREALTIME is a smoke alarm. When unemployment rises too fast, it says: "Uh oh… something may be going wrong."

Liquidity — Why is the market still rising?

Fed Balance Sheet
WALCL — total assets (trillion $)
--
loading

WALCL is America's money tap — the Fed's total balance sheet. Is the Fed printing money, or pulling money out of the system?

The Fed buys: government bonds 🇺🇸, mortgage bonds 🏠, emergency bank loans 🏦.
Buying more → more liquidity. Selling/stopping → money leaves.

Think of a garden 🌱

📈 Balance growing = more water 💧 = markets get support.
📉 Balance shrinking = less water 🚿 = harder for stocks and crypto.

Two terms to know:

QE (Quantitative Easing) — Fed buys a lot, pumps money in. Did this during 2008 and Covid → balance went from ~$4T to ~$9T.
QT (Quantitative Tightening) — opposite. Balance shrinks slowly. Since 2022 peak it has come down significantly.

The big analogy:

Interest rates = gas pedal/brake 🚗
Fed balance sheet = how much fuel in the tank ⛽
You can press the gas, but without fuel you won't get far 😄

💡 WALCL shows whether the Fed is pumping money in or pulling it out. More money = usually good for stocks and crypto.

M2 Money Supply
YoY % — negative = tight
--
loading

M2SL is America's money-in-circulation meter. How much money is swimming around in the US economy?

Think of America as one giant swimming pool 🏊 — M2 measures how much "usable money" is in it: cash 💵, checking accounts, savings, money market accounts, small bank deposits.

How do you read it?

M2 rising fast 📈 → more money → often bullish for risk assets.
M2 growing slowly → normal economy.
M2 falling 📉 → liquidity removed → headwind for stocks and crypto.

For stocks & crypto:

M2 up = often good for Bitcoin, Nasdaq, growth stocks. Extra money seeks returns and flows into tech, AI, crypto.
⚠️ Note the lag: M2 changes first → markets react months later.

Difference from WALCL:

WALCL = water from the tap 🚿 (what the Fed does).
M2 = how much water is in the pool 🏊 (what's actually circulating).

💡 More money circulating = often good for stocks and crypto. Less money = tougher times for risk assets.

Fed Funds Rate
Effective policy rate (%)
--
loading

FEDFUNDS is arguably the most important economic indicator of all — America's interest rate dial. How expensive is it to borrow money?

The Fed is behind the wheel of the US economy. The rate is their main control: give gas (lower) or brake (raise). It influences almost all other rates — mortgages 🏠, car loans 🚗, credit cards 💳, business loans 🏭, savings 💰.

How do you read it?

Low (0%–2%) → cheap to borrow → people buy homes, invest → often good for Nasdaq, tech and crypto 🚀
Medium (3%–5%) → normal economy.
High (5%+) → expensive → headwind for growth stocks and crypto 📉

The golden rule:

Lower rates = usually bullish for tech and crypto — savings pay little, so money flows into Nasdaq, AI, Bitcoin.
Higher rates = usually bearish — if savings pay 5%, why take big risks?

💡 FEDFUNDS is America's rate dial. Low rates accelerate stocks and crypto. High rates are the brake.
